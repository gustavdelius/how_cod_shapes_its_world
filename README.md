# How cod shapes its world: a mizer model

A two-species [mizer](https://sizespectrum.org/mizer/) size-spectrum model of
cod and sprat. It reproduces the **emergent Allee effect** described by
Van Leeuwen, De Roos & Persson (2008), *How cod shapes its world*, Journal of
Sea Research 60: 89–104 ([doi:10.1016/j.seares.2008.02.008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.seares.2008.02.008)).

**Read the document:** <https://gustavdelius.github.io/how_cod_shapes_its_world/>.

## The mechanism

Cod predation thins the sprat population. The surviving sprat have more food,
so they grow and reproduce faster and produce many small sprat. Small sprat are
exactly the food that juvenile cod need. Without cod, sprat become a stunted
population that produces few young, and newly settled cod starve even though
total prey biomass is high. Cod therefore do well at high density but cannot
increase from low density. The community has two alternative stable states,
and a collapsed cod stock need not recover when fishing stops.

## What the model shows

With identical parameters, and with no imposed stock–recruitment relation
(`R_max = Inf`), the model has two stable steady states:

| | With cod | Without cod |
|:--|--:|--:|
| Sprat recruitment | 4.7× higher | baseline |
| Sprat biomass below 0.1 g | 4.1× higher | baseline |
| Feeding level of 0.5 g cod (critical level 0.16) | 0.25 | 0.15 |
| Invasion fitness R0 of cod | 1.0 | 0 |

The document also shows:

- **An Allee threshold.** Cod introduced into the cod-free state establish only
  at 30 % or more of their equilibrium abundance.
- **Collapse without recovery.** Cod collapse under rising fishing and do not
  return after fishing stops, even with constant immigration.
- **Hysteresis.** The cod state disappears at a fishing mortality of about
  0.39/yr, and lowering fishing afterwards does not bring it back.
- **Recovery by prey thinning.** A sprat fishery lasting 30 years lets cod
  return for good; 20 years is not enough. The paper proposed this management
  option.

The model does not reproduce every detail of the paper. The document lists the
differences, including the fairly high cod mortality needed for a stable cod
state and a different size pattern of cod predation on sprat.

## Reproducing

Requirements:

- R with the packages ggplot2, patchwork, knitr and nleqslv (used by mizer's
  Newton steady-state solver)
- The development version of mizer. The results were computed with mizer
  3.4.0.9001:

  ```r
  remotes::install_github("sizespectrum/mizer")
  ```

- The [Quarto](https://quarto.org) command-line tool

Build the two steady states and save them to `model/`:

```bash
Rscript R/build_model.R
```

Render the document to `docs/index.html` (a few minutes on the first run;
knitr caches the results afterwards):

```bash
quarto render
```

GitHub Pages publishes `docs/` once the repository's Settings → Pages is set
to deploy from the `main` branch, folder `/docs`. Commit the re-rendered
`docs/index.html` to update the published page.

To load the model in an R session:

```r
library(mizer)
params_cod <- readParams("model/cod_sprat_with_cod.rds")
params_no_cod <- readParams("model/cod_sprat_no_cod.rds")
```

## Known issue

Long projections with the second-order van Leer flux
(`second_order_w(params) <- c(flux = "van_leer")`) currently produce `NaN`
after about 250 simulated years, because of a mizer bug reported as
[sizespectrum/mizer#630](https://github.com/sizespectrum/mizer/issues/630).
The document's van Leer check therefore uses mizer's Newton solver instead of
a projection. The default upwind scheme used everywhere else is not affected.
